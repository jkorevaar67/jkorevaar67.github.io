Rough graphs

Describe changes in a graph and sketch graphs from a situation.

What can you tell without numbers?

7 min · Describe the graph to a partner. Start on the left. What changes and what stays the same?

Choose a term to highlight its section.
A rough graph shows how two variables are related. Its axes are labelled and usually have no numbers. You can describe how a variable changes and compare heights or speeds shown on the same graph.

Read Wouter’s speed graph

9 min · For each part, decide what happens to Wouter’s speed as time moves forward.

Match the shape to its graph

10 min · Water flows into each container at a steady rate. Think about what happens to the water height when the container gets wider or narrower.

Extension · Sketch five more shapes

Sketch one water-height graph per container on paper. Start each graph at the origin. Use the selector to compare a sketch with its model.

Sketch Carolien’s cycle ride

14 min · Carolien cycles slowly, waits at a level crossing, sets off quickly and cycles fast. At school she brakes hard and parks her bike. Sketch both graphs, then compare each with a model.

Drag to sketch. With a keyboard, focus the graph and use the arrow keys to move, Enter to add a point and Escape to start a new line.

Teacher notes

7 min discovery · 9 min scooter graph · 10 min water graphs · 14 min sketch and exit question. Have pupils explain a choice before checking it. The extra water sketches are an extension. Accept alternative sketches that show the same changes in speed and distance.
