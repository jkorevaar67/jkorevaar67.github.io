Negative numbers

Learn how negative numbers work, then challenge yourself with interactive questions.

Based on textbook § 2.4, pages 73–79. Examples and interactive questions are newly written.

Choose your theory

Theory A · Positive and negative numbers

Numbers greater than 0 are positive. Numbers smaller than 0 are negative. Zero is neither positive nor negative.

On a number line, moving right makes numbers larger. Moving left makes numbers smaller.

Example: −3 < 2 because −3 is to the left of 2. The opposite of −5 is 5, and the opposite of 0 is 0.

Remember: Opposite numbers add up to zero: −7 + 7 = 0.

Theory B · Arrows on a number line

To add a positive number, move right. To subtract a positive number, move left.
Example 1: −3 + 5 = 2. Start at −3, move five steps right.

Example 2: −1 − 7 = −8. Start at −1, move seven steps left.

Theory C · Adding negative numbers

Adding a negative number has the same effect as subtracting its positive opposite.

8 + (−3) = 8 − 3 = 5

Worked examples

−9 + (−3) = −12

15 + (−16) = −1

−5 + 8 = 3

Tip: If both numbers are negative, add their sizes and keep the minus sign.

Theory D · Subtracting negative numbers

Subtracting a negative number is the same as adding its positive opposite.

15 − (−4) = 15 + 4 = 19

Worked examples

8 − (−3) = 8 + 3 = 11

−13 − (−27) = −13 + 27 = 14

−16 − (−2 − 8) = −16 − (−10) = −6

Remember: Work out brackets first. Then change subtraction of a negative number into addition.
