Your learning goal

Discover patterns in the coordinates of a sequence of points and use them to find points that are not drawn.

BasicRead a coordinate table, find constant steps and continue simple patterns.
IntermediateUse negative numbers and halves. Jump forwards or backwards and find a figure number.
ChallengeChanging steps, doubling, growing houses and triangles, and the vertices of nested squares.
Theory A · Patterns and coordinates

Look at x and y separately

A table makes a coordinate pattern easier to spot. First compare the x-values. Then compare the y-values. They do not have to follow the same rule.

x goes up by 2 each time.
y goes up by 1 each time.
The next two points are (9, 5) and (11, 6).
  1. Put the figure numbers and coordinates into a table.
  2. Find the change in x and the change in y.
  3. Check your rule against every point in the table.
  4. Use it to calculate the point you need.

Each figure is numbered at its highest point.

Practise simple patterns →
Basic

Continue a pattern

For (−2, 4), (1, 2), (4, 0), (7, −2), x increases by 3 and y decreases by 2. The next point is (10, −4).

Use the words increase by and decrease by to describe the rule. Always check both coordinates.

Intermediate

Jump forwards and backwards

In the triangle pattern above, each next highest point moves 2 right and 1 up. Triangle 80 has highest point (159, 80). Triangle 85 is 5 steps further along.

x = 159 + 5 × 2 = 169
y = 80 + 5 × 1 = 85
So triangle 85 has highest point (169, 85).

To go backwards, reverse the changes. Triangle 77 is 3 steps earlier: (159 − 3 × 2, 80 − 3 × 1) = (153, 77).

Negative and half-unit coordinates

Here x decreases by 2 and y decreases by ½. From figure 3 at (−1, −2) to figure 6 is three steps: (−7, −3½).

Challenge

The steps can change

Do not assume that every pattern has a constant step.

(−1, 2), (−6, 4), (−11, 8), (−16, 16)
x decreases by 5, but y doubles.
The next point is (−21, 32).

Growing houses

The y-coordinate is the house number. The x-increases are 1½, 2½, 3½, …. The next increase is 4½, giving house 5 at (9½, 5).

For this house pattern, the next x-increase is the current house number + ½. From house 10 at (47, 10), add 10½ to get house 11 at (57½, 11).

Triangles with changing widths

The leftmost points move right by 1, then 2, then 3, … while y decreases by 1. Figure 5 has leftmost point (10, −4).

A pattern growing to the left

The x-coordinate decreases by 2, then 3, then 4, … while y increases by 1. The next highest point is (−12, 5).

Nested squares

The smallest square runs from (1, 1) to (2, 2). Each new square extends one unit in every direction. List its vertices in this order: bottom left, bottom right, top right, top left.

Square 3: (−1, −1), (4, −1), (4, 4), (−1, 4).
Square 5: (−3, −3), (6, −3), (6, 6), (−3, 6).

From square n to the next square, subtract 1 from each lower boundary and add 1 to each upper boundary. Use the number of steps to jump directly to a much larger square.

Check your method

  • Can you explain the x-rule and y-rule separately?
  • Can you find a distant point without drawing every figure?
  • Can you reverse the pattern?
  • Do the steps stay the same, or do they change?
