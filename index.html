📘 Learn · Prime Factorisation

Discover the ideas before you start practising.

1. What is a factor?

A factor divides a number exactly, with no remainder.

Example: 3 is a factor of 12 because 12 ÷ 3 = 4.

The factors of 12 are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 and 12.

2. What is a prime number?

A prime number is a whole number greater than 1 with exactly two positive factors: 1 and itself.

Examples: 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13. Remember: 1 is not prime.

3. Prime factorisation

Break a number into a multiplication of prime numbers. Divide by the smallest possible prime each time.

24 = 2 × 12 = 2 × 2 × 6 = 2 × 2 × 2 × 3

In the exercise, type 2x2x2x3. Do not use powers.

4. Highest Common Factor (HCF)

Find the prime factors shared by all numbers and multiply them.

12 = 2 × 2 × 3
18 = 2 × 3 × 3

Shared prime factors: 2 × 3, so HCF = 6.

If there are no shared prime factors, the HCF is 1.

5. Lowest Common Multiple (LCM)

Use each prime factor as many times as needed to make both numbers.

12 = 2 × 2 × 3
18 = 2 × 3 × 3

Take 2 × 2 × 3 × 3, so LCM = 36.
